Below lists what I am most passionate about for this experience

- player can decide how to build their village
- player can evolve their village to a city
- player can customize their people/village
- player define their level of challenge organically
	(making intentional choices that lead to more or less challenging experiences)

- The growth system should be simple, so we only have 2 resources
	- Materials = Building / Customizing the village
	- Supplies = Creating / Customizing the villagers

-If the game is about growth here are interesting challenges the player may be up against
	-> limited resources = {literal resources, competition from others, sustain costs}
	-> which way to grow = {improve what we have, learn to do more}
	-> gathering choices = {collecting, stealing, questing, conquering}
	-> relationships = {helping others survive, getting special nature events for finding/protecting an area}
	

- Prototype/Demo
	- focused on single player proof of concept
	- map should be big enough to showcase one or two of each element (doesnt need to be huge)
	- should feel like a tiny living world
	- units should have starchly different customization options
		(players should be able to make body types through sliders like dwarves vs elfs)
	- buildings should have simple different options (like material/texture presets we can swap between)
	- custom colors for units and buildings is important (skin color, and a 2-tier clothing pattern)
	- upgrades should feel like playstyle choices
		(not anyone choice is default best, and each supports a specific playstyle) 

- Player Experience
	- Average session: 75 minutes (45-100m more or less is okay if its still fun and uncommon)
	- Emotions: Curious (to try things), Excited (to beat, fix, solve challenges), Rewarded (for having their gameplay choices provided respectable results)
	- Setting: Fictional Fantasy (start like caveman version, potentially grow to scifi)
	- Controlled Context: Players can build/control units, multiple buildings
	- A progression system based on perks (can be named something else) that defines the civilizations strengths
		(examples can be faster units, faster building, faster collecting, faster learning perks, tougher/healthier units, etc)
	- Gameplay Loop:
			1) Customize / Choose
			2) Explore / Discover
			3) React / Collect / Destroy / Protect
			4) Benefit / Profit / Progress (now time to spend those profits on #1)
	- Rough Simulation
			1) Customize base unit that all units will look similar too & choose a base perk
			2) Players will load into a map only able to see around where their units start
			3) With around 3 units at start they can choose to Build, Collect, Explore, Combat
	- Unit Progression
			- Most units will remain standard, but in certain situations units will gain enough experience to evolve and become larger / better and even get a nickname the player can change
			- on progression it would be nice if they were offered choices based on what they have experience doing, meaning each unit should track what experience it has
			- units can slowly grow automatically by repeating tasks, but significant growth should happen from large milestones that are not common to hit
			
	

- Scenario Examples
	- Player A
		- wants to make their character look like orcs from dnd, and wants to build a grand army that can conquer nature and other civilizations
	- Player B
		- wants to make gray gnomes that invent machinery to collect resources so they end up progressing technology faster
	- Player C
		- wants to make druid like elves who build a bond with nature and gain units and special rewards from the environment

   

Suggestion for a First Prototype

Given your interests and experience level:
	- Focus your first prototype around building, resource flow, and player-defined pacing.
	- Skip combat initially — just have the world “react” (maybe small threats, weather, or growth challenges).
	- Add your “peaceful vs. aggressive” toggle as a simple global modifier.
	- Build out from there.
That gives you a satisfying core that you can later expand into RTS, survival, or sandbox directions — whichever feels best in playtesting.
